Upstream

You are the new program officer at the Riverbend Community Health Foundation. Overdose deaths are climbing, violence is up, and the gap in life expectancy between the county's richest and poorest neighborhoods is 14 years.

You have six years and about $10 million a year. Every program helps, but on different timelines. Your board wants results it can see. Your community needs results that last.

DownstreamTreat harm after it happens. Results this year, but they fade when funding stops.
MidstreamReach people at risk. Results in about a year.
UpstreamChange the conditions that create risk. Slow to start, broad, and they compound.

About 10 minutes. Riverbend is fictional and the model is illustrative, built to show the shape of real trade-offs.

What this game is getting at

Lags are real. Housing and early childhood investments look like they do nothing for two or three years. Then they move every outcome at once, and the gains keep building.
Downstream is not wasted. Naloxone and treatment save lives this year, and those people need to be alive to benefit from upstream change. The strongest portfolios usually blend tiers.
Accountability shapes strategy. Boards and funders who expect annual wins push money downstream. Protecting patient, upstream money is one of the hardest jobs in philanthropy.
Stopping matters. Downstream effects fade quickly when funding ends. Upstream effects persist, which is why the projection years reward them.
